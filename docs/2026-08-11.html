EverQuote buys into AI shopping distribution stack

EverQuote entered an exclusive commercial partnership and took a minority stake in Waniwani, a 13-person startup that builds AI distribution tools for ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, to integrate into its offerings for P&C carriers and agents.

So whatDistribution is being redefined by agentic shopping, and carriers who have no presence inside AI assistants risk losing quote volume to whoever gets discoverability right first.
DeeperThe headline number is the stake itself, undisclosed in size, but the more telling figures sit in the background: EverQuote's web traffic has been cut in half over the past year according to Coverager's Monthly Innovation Report, even as Q2 revenue hit $195.1 million, up 25%, with adjusted EBITDA of $30.1 million, up 37%, mostly from auto at $172.1 million versus home at $23 million. That traffic collapse is the real driver behind this deal, not growth confidence. Waniwani raised an $8 million seed led by Seedcamp in June and already powers Tuio, a Lemonade-like MGA that is quoting home insurance directly inside ChatGPT. The irony is that EverQuote also runs ZestyQuote, which nominally routes to Lemonade, so it is now investing in the same infrastructure that could disintermediate its own funnel. CEO Jayme Mendal frames this as staying visible in agentic AI channels, but nothing here proves that being 'discoverable' inside an AI assistant preserves carrier pricing control or margin the way a branded search result does. What is unproven is whether consumers using ChatGPT to shop insurance behave anything like consumers using a comparison site, and whether carriers gain or lose leverage once an AI agent controls the comparison.
Watch next: Whether Lemonade or another direct carrier launches inside ChatGPT to test Tuio's early lead in agentic quoting.

DeeperThe supplied text is announcement-level only, with no architecture, model provider, timeline, use cases, or investment figures disclosed. What is not yet known is whether Allie touches underwriting, claims, or servicing, and what governance wraps around agent decisions and hand-offs to humans. That gap matters because everything else in today's set, from Vertafore's Reconciliation Agent to Sapiens leaning on Claude, is carriers and vendors buying or renting agentic capability rather than building it. Allstate diverging toward a build strategy is the notable signal, but it should be treated as a build-in-progress claim from trade press rather than a confirmed capability. The strategic question this raises for any carrier is whether proprietary agent orchestration becomes a durable moat or simply a slower, more expensive path to the same endpoint vendors already sell.

Vertafore adds AI agent for carrier statement reconciliation

Vertafore launched a Reconciliation Agent inside its Velocity AI Platform that automatically matches carrier statements against agency transactions across more than 200 carrier formats, following its earlier Insurance Expert Agent and Email Agent.

So whatThis targets a real cost center in commission and payables reconciliation, and if it performs as described it shortens month-end close without touching pricing, underwriting, or claims decisions, which makes it an easier governance approval than most agentic AI pitches.
DeeperThe agent analyzes transaction data, proposes matches, and surfaces exceptions such as expected commission discrepancies, rather than making autonomous decisions, which keeps it in the assistive rather than agentic-decisioning category despite the branding. Vertafore positions the value as freeing accounting teams from line-by-line manual matching as agency transaction volume grows. No accuracy rate, error rate, or named customer deployment is given in the text, so every efficiency claim here is vendor description rather than measured outcome. This sits well below the risk profile of underwriting or claims automation stories elsewhere in today's set, since a bad match in reconciliation is a finance control issue, not a pricing or coverage decision. The practical value for a carrier's governance team is that back-office reconciliation is a low-regret category to pilot precisely because errors are caught downstream in financial controls rather than surfaced to policyholders.
