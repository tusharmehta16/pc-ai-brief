SCOR expands AI use while flagging its own liability exposure

SCOR is increasing its use of AI internally while its leadership publicly warned about governance and liability challenges the technology creates for the reinsurer.

So whatWhen a top five reinsurer flags AI liability risk on its own book, primary carriers ceding to that paper should expect tighter treaty language and disclosure requirements around AI-assisted underwriting and claims decisions.
DeeperThe reporting is thin, essentially a single headline, so the specific use cases and the exact nature of the liability concern SCOR raised are not disclosed. What is notable is the juxtaposition itself: a reinsurer is simultaneously scaling AI adoption and warning about governance gaps, which typically precedes changes to treaty wording, capacity terms, or disclosure asks aimed at cedants. This connects directly to the Marsh Re and Moody's items below: three separate voices in the risk transfer chain are now treating AI as something that needs its own governance and pricing treatment rather than a generic efficiency tool. What remains unproven is whether SCOR's warning translates into concrete treaty changes or stays at the level of public commentary. A carrier with SCOR or peer reinsurance exposure should ask its reinsurance and actuarial teams whether any cedant-facing AI governance questions have already surfaced in renewal discussions. If they have not yet, they likely will at the next renewal cycle given how public this framing now is.

Marsh Re builds named-peril cover tied to AI catastrophe risk

Marsh Re has developed a named-peril reinsurance cover connected to AI and catastrophe risk.

So whatThis signals the market moving from AI as an underwriting tool to AI itself, or AI-dependent infrastructure, becoming an insurable named peril class with its own capacity and pricing.
DeeperThe available text is headline-only, so the exact structure is not confirmed: it could be a cat cover that uses AI models to price named perils more precisely, or it could be a cover that treats AI-related catastrophic failure, such as a data center or algorithmic outage, as the insured peril itself. That distinction matters enormously for how a carrier should read this. If it is the former, it is a modeling upgrade with modest structural implications. If it is the latter, it is the first sign of a genuinely new peril class entering the reinsurance market, parallel to how cyber emerged as a standalone line two decades ago. Given the same week's Benzinga piece framing AI data center exposure as a potential 200 billion dollar market, the more consequential reading leans toward AI infrastructure risk being packaged as an insurable peril. What is not known yet is capacity size, pricing basis, or which primary carriers are cedants on this cover. This is worth flagging to the reinsurance purchasing team now, before terms firm up at the next renewal.

Moody's ranks AI alongside cyber and physical risk as top threat

Moody's assessment states that physical risk, cyber and AI are among the most severe emerging threats redefining the insurance industry.

So whatA rating agency placing AI in the same severity tier as cyber and physical catastrophe means AI exposure will increasingly factor into how carriers are assessed on capital adequacy and risk management maturity, not just innovation scorecards.
DeeperThe source is a summary headline without the underlying report detail, so the specific mechanisms Moody's cites for AI as a severe threat, whether that is underwriting model risk, liability accumulation, operational dependency, or something else, are not given here. What can be read into the framing is the sequencing: Moody's is grouping AI with two risk categories, cyber and physical catastrophe, that already carry explicit capital charges and stress test requirements in most rating agency and regulatory capital frameworks. If AI follows that trajectory, boards and CROs should expect rating agencies to start asking pointed questions about AI governance frameworks during the next review cycle, similar to how cyber resilience questions became standard practice after cyber was elevated to a top tier risk. This is consistent with the SCOR governance warning above, both point toward AI graduating from a project-level concern to an enterprise risk category. What is unproven is whether Moody's has published or will publish a scoring methodology that carriers can act against, or whether this remains directional commentary for now.
