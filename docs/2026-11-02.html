Five Sigma and Amarillo target London Market claims data

Five Sigma and Amarillo Technologies announced a partnership combining AI claims automation with bordereaux and delegated authority data management for the London Market.

So whatCarriers writing delegated authority or binder business get a signal that claims automation vendors are now targeting the messiest part of that book, the bordereaux data feed, rather than just the claims desk.
DeeperFive Sigma positions itself as an AI native claims platform built around Clive, a system of AI agents meant to run tasks across the full claims lifecycle from first notice of loss to settlement, according to CTO Michael Krikheli. Amarillo brings its Fusion platform, which automates collection, processing and validation of bordereaux data for insurers, MGAs and brokers. Krikheli frames the problem bluntly: delegated authority is where fragmented data and manual processes create the most friction, not only in London but globally. Amarillo Managing Director Zoe Steele frames the deal as letting her customers access claims expertise alongside their existing data work. No carrier or MGA client is named as having adopted the combined offering, and no efficiency numbers are given, so this is a go to market tie up rather than a proven deployment. The real test for London Market carriers is whether cleaner bordereaux data actually lets Clive automate more claims decisions without human review, which is the harder and unproven half of the pitch. This connects to the broader question raised elsewhere today about whether oversight of AI agents is keeping pace with how much autonomy they are being given in claims and underwriting workflows.
Watch next: Whether Five Sigma or Amarillo name a live carrier or MGA client running the combined claims and bordereaux workflow.

Manulife puts AI underwriting guidance in advisers' hands

Manulife launched an Advisor AI Assistant that gives advisers continuous access to AI generated underwriting guidance before they submit an application.

So whatThis moves AI from back office triage into the point of sale, shaping what advisers believe a risk will underwrite as before the case ever reaches an underwriter.
DeeperThe available detail is limited to the launch itself: advisers get round the clock access to AI generated underwriting guidance ahead of submission. What is not stated is whether the guidance is binding, how it is validated against actual underwriting outcomes, or what happens when the assistant's guidance turns out to be wrong and a case is declined or rerated after the adviser and client expected otherwise. Manulife is primarily a life and wealth carrier, but the mechanic, using a language model to pre screen risk appetite before formal submission, is directly transferable to commercial P&C lines where brokers currently guess at appetite before quoting. If this becomes standard practice, carriers need a clear answer on who owns the gap between AI guidance and the final underwriting decision, since that gap is where adviser and broker trust, and potential complaints, will concentrate. Nothing in the announcement addresses model governance, data lineage, or an audit trail for the guidance given, which are the questions a regulator would ask first.
Watch next: Whether Manulife or a peer publishes any detail on guardrails, audit trail, or error rate for AI generated underwriting guidance.
