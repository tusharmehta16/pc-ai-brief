Moody's finds AI deployment outruns ability to prove ROI

Moody's surveyed 58 insurers, reinsurers and brokers and found about two thirds running AI broadly or across multiple production use cases, but only 41% quantify and consistently track benefits per use case.

So whatSpend is scaling faster than the instrumentation needed to defend it, which is exactly the governance gap that turns into budget scrutiny or a credit flag the next time results disappoint.
DeeperThe headline framing, AI is mainstream, is not the interesting number. The sharper read is the measurement gap: 74% report faster cycle times and 76% report productivity gains or headcount avoidance, yet only 41% quantify and track benefits per use case, 34% quantify without tracking, and 24% rely on qualitative judgement only. In P&C specifically, operations leads with 75% in production, underwriting and pricing and claims and fraud detection are each past 50%, but catastrophe and exposure management sits at just 19%, the one area most tied to tail risk and capital. P&C carriers are also spending harder than life insurers, with nearly one in five above 10% of IT budget versus 7% for life, and 44% in the 5 to 10% band versus 3% for life. The named obstacles are skills and talent (81%), data quality (71%), legacy integration (62%) and regulatory uncertainty across jurisdictions (47%). Moody's conclusion is that the credit effect is neutral to positive but entirely conditional on execution, data quality and governance, and that AI itself increases operational and regulatory risk even as it reduces manual process risk. 88% say their governance aligns with regulatory guidance, which is a self assessment, not an audit finding.
Watch next: Whether any surveyed carriers publish a formal ROI tracking cadence per use case, which would be the first hard evidence that the measurement gap is closing rather than widening.

MGT lets agents quote through ChatGPT and Claude via MCP

MGT Insurance, a Colorado based carrier with $41.6M in total funding, says independent agents can now chat or upload documents inside ChatGPT and Claude to obtain a policy, enabled through a Model Context Protocol integration, and claims to be first to offer this on both platforms.

So whatThis is a live precedent for binding insurance activity mediated by a third party LLM chat interface, and it tests whether a carrier's distribution controls are ready for that channel before a competitor or a regulator forces the question.
DeeperThe mechanics are specific even if the risk controls are not: MCP is the protocol that lets an external AI assistant call out to a carrier's systems, so ChatGPT or Claude becomes a front end to MGT's quoting flow rather than just an advisory tool. What the announcement does not say is which products are available, how agent identity is authenticated inside the chat session, or what controls exist before a quote can actually bind. That absence matters more than the launch itself, because it means the operational and E&O exposure of this channel is currently undocumented in public, not just the vendor pitch. This is the kind of frontier distribution move that shows up as a competitive feature in RFPs well before anyone has stress tested it for errors, omissions, or dispute handling when a binding action happens inside a model neither the carrier nor the agent fully controls.
Watch next: Whether MGT or any peer discloses authentication, quoting, and binding safeguards for the MCP channel, or whether a regulator asks the question first.
