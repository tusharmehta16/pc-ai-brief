Chubb CEO says AI liability is unpriced casualty exposure

On Chubb's Q3 earnings call, Evan Greenberg said AI liability is a growing exposure that is not contemplated in casualty underwriting today.

So whatAny carrier with casualty books is currently silent on AI exposure, meaning claims from AI-driven decisions or failures are landing on policies never priced for them.
DeeperGreenberg's exact framing matters more than the headline suggests: he said the industry has to put its arms around AI liability the way it did with emerging liabilities over the last 50 years, and he called this a 'pretty good template to follow,' not a crisis. He expects the fix to be a combination of coverage clarifications, exclusions, and separate AI-related liability coverages, developed alongside underwriting and pricing that discerns between insureds. He also floated certification standards and best practices for AI and agentics use that could feed directly into underwriting criteria, similar to cyber maturity scoring. Notably, he described Chubb's own internal controls on agentics and LLMs, including limiting what systems can do, where, and how, plus layered defense against external misuse of agentics targeting Chubb's own data. What remains unproven is timing and mechanics: no exclusion language, no pricing model, and no certification standard exists yet, this is a CEO signaling direction, not a filed product. This connects directly to the human review and governance theme elsewhere in today's set, since 'control and limit what they can do' is the same logic underpinning liability-shield arguments for human-in-the-loop review.
Watch next: Watch for the first AI liability exclusion or endorsement filing from a major casualty carrier or ISO standard form.
