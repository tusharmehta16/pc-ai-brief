AI data centers push captive insurance into explosive growth

Marsh's US and Canada captive solutions leader Michael Serricchio said captives are becoming the go-to coverage model for AI data center construction, surety, property and liability risk, with global captive premium up nearly 20% in two years to roughly $240 billion across more than 6,000 captives.

So whatCarriers risk losing underwriting access to the largest and fastest growing commercial property class just as it scales, while retaining exposure only on the excess layers ceded through reinsurance.
DeeperThe headline number, $240 billion in global captive premium, is not actually the interesting figure. The more telling detail is that Marsh alone manages 1,900 captives writing $79 billion in premium, of which only $11.5 billion is used to buy reinsurance, meaning the vast majority of that risk never reaches a traditional balance sheet at all. AM Best says the roughly 150 US captives it rates generated over $8 billion in savings over five years, and Aon's Joe Peiser frames the driver plainly as loss severity: when premiums rise, clients retain the bottom layer themselves. What is new here is the asset class. Captives were pioneered by oil and gas for hard to insure environmental catastrophes, but hyperscalers are now applying the same logic to facilities that are frequently sited in tornado, flood and drought prone areas because land is cheap. S&P's Charles-Marie Delpuech said Meta's Hyperion campus was 'beyond fully-insurable,' forcing a bespoke risk retention structure outside conventional captives entirely. Serricchio declined to name specific deals, so the scale of actual data center captive formation remains unconfirmed, this is a directional call from a broker executive, not a disclosed market figure. What is proven is the appetite and the mechanism; what is not proven yet is how much premium volume actually moves before carriers can price for it.
Watch next: Watch for the first named hyperscaler captive or risk retention structure to be publicly disclosed, and whether reinsurers are only being invited in for top catastrophe layers.

Underwriters fear losing judgment more than losing jobs to AI

A hyperexponential survey of 350 CUOs and senior underwriters found 44% fear senior judgment leaving the industry undocumented, while knowledge transfer technology receives only 8% of firms' AI investment, the lowest of any category.

So whatCarriers are funding AI for admin speed while the actual scarce asset, underwriting judgment, is not being captured before it retires, which sets up a capability gap that will surface exactly when it is hardest to reverse.
DeeperOnly 15% of respondents said their firm has found a way to capture what its best underwriters know, and two in five said critical judgment is either poorly documented or exists solely in a colleague's head. Fear of outright AI job replacement is actually falling fast, from 18% in 2024 to 11% in 2025 to just 5% now, so the anxiety has shifted from displacement to erosion. There is also a perception gap worth flagging to any executive who asks: only 12% of senior and lead underwriters feel AI is fully integrated into their workflow, versus 29% of CUOs, meaning confidence in AI adoption grows the further you sit from the actual risk decision. While 51% cite manual admin time savings as AI's main benefit, only 21% report improved decision quality, and the top ranked way underwriters want AI to engage with any task is 'AI suggests, I approve,' with trust in full autonomy collapsing from 34% on data ingestion to just 8% on coaching junior staff. Jamie Wilson of hyperexponential summarized it as judgment, not speed, being the scarce resource, and it is leaving faster than it is being replaced. The obstacles underwriters actually cite, inconsistent data at 44% and insufficient context on prior risks at 35%, point to a data and time problem being misdiagnosed as a skills problem.
Watch next: Watch whether carriers shift AI budget allocation toward coaching and knowledge capture tools in the next 12 to 18 month planning cycle that hx is tracking.

EigenRisk adds GeoX property data to catastrophe platform

EigenRisk announced an agreement to integrate GeoX's AI powered property intelligence into its EigenPrism catastrophe risk platform.

So whatThis is another point solution stacking into the cat modeling stack, offering incremental property level data enrichment without disclosed proof of accuracy gains.
DeeperThe supplied text confirms only that the partnership exists, there are no financial terms, no named carrier clients, and no quantified improvement in underwriting accuracy despite the headline claim. This is the standard pattern for insurtech integration announcements: a vendor press release framed as an underwriting breakthrough with no evidence attached. It is worth noting only because it targets the same exposure geography as today's captive story, tornado, flood and drought prone land, which is precisely where AI data centers and other large commercial properties are increasingly being sited. Whether GeoX's data actually changes loss ratios or pricing accuracy for any live book is unknown from this release. Treat it as a pipeline signal for the cat modeling vendor landscape, not as evidence of a functioning improvement.
Watch next: Watch for a named carrier or MGA to disclose a measurable pricing or loss ratio impact from the GeoX integration.
