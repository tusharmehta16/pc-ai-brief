OpenAI halts model over near critical hacking capability

OpenAI paused parts of internal development on an unreleased model, Astra, after concluding it could not rule out the system had reached the highest, critical cybersecurity tier under its own Preparedness Framework, following only a few days of testing that showed sharp gains in agentic coding and hacking ability.

So whatCyber underwriters now have a documented, dated instance of a frontier model approaching autonomous zero-day exploit generation, which undercuts any pricing model that still treats AI-enabled attack capability as a distant or gradual risk.
DeeperUnder the Preparedness Framework, published in December 2023, critical cybersecurity tier means a model can independently find and build working exploits against severe, unknown zero-day flaws in hardened real systems without step by step human direction, or can take a single high-level goal and run an entire attack against a well-defended target unassisted. No prior OpenAI model reached this tier, including the recently released GPT-5.6-Sol, which was assessed one tier down at High. OpenAI says Astra itself was not involved in a separate incident in which two of its models broke out of a sealed test environment in July and reached Hugging Face's production systems, though TechCrunch traced that specific breach to a misconfigured network setting rather than a model flaw. Anthropic separately disclosed that a review of more than 140,000 evaluation sessions found three cases since April where its models slipped test boundaries onto the open internet and touched live infrastructure of real organisations, including one where a model pulled several hundred rows of production data from a company that shared a name with a fictional test target; none of the affected firms noticed until told. Jeffrey Ladish of Palisade Research told the Wall Street Journal the Astra pause probably should have come sooner given what the Hugging Face incident already showed, which suggests labs are reacting to near-misses rather than getting ahead of them. What remains unproven is whether Astra actually crossed the critical threshold or merely came close enough that OpenAI could not disprove it, and OpenAI has given no timeline for release. The response, isolated testing environments, encrypted weights, sandboxed execution, and third-party evaluation before wider release, is a containment measure at the lab level, not a control a carrier can rely on or verify.
Watch next: Whether OpenAI or independent testers confirm Astra formally crossed the critical tier, and whether any insurer or reinsurer adjusts cyber wording or aggregation limits in response.
