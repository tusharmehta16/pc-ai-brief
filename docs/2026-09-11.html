NAIC to require insurers disclose AI use in claims and models

NAIC signaled it will require insurers to show how they use AI in claims handling and in underwriting or pricing models.

So whatThis turns internal model inventories and AI documentation from a best practice into an examinable compliance artifact that regulators can request on demand.
DeeperThe supplied text is a headline only, so the mechanics are not yet known: whether this arrives as a model bulletin, a model law, or an extension of the AI governance framework several states have already adopted, and what the compliance timeline looks like. What is clear from the framing is the direction, which is disclosure of specific AI use tied to specific decisions rather than a general attestation of AI principles. That is a materially higher bar than most carriers' current governance maturity, given Accenture's finding elsewhere in today's set that only 23 percent of insurers have scaled AI enterprise wide. It also connects to the separate Insurance Day framing of a tightening regulatory net on AI and liability, though that piece offers no named regulator or binding action yet. For a portfolio leader, the open question is whether your current model inventory can be produced in the format a supervisor would actually request. Nothing here says the requirement is final or dated, so treat this as a signal to prepare rather than a deadline to hit.

Anthropic documents AI accelerated attacks and blocked bioweapons research

Anthropic's September 2026 threat report details AI assisted cyberattacks and five blocked attempts at AI assisted biological weapons research between December 2025 and August 2026.

So whatThis gives cyber and life sciences underwriters primary source case evidence that attacker speed and reach are rising even as cyber premiums fell, widening the gap between price and risk.
DeeperThe specifics matter more than the headline. A group consistent with Russia linked Midnight Blizzard built AI workflows that detected when their malware was flagged and rewrote the code until it evaded detection, and separately compromised hotel WiFi at three hospitality vendors through DNS hijacking to target government and defence workers. ShinyHunters affiliates moved from initial access to bulk theft in as little as two to three hours, exfiltrating more than a terabyte from one technology provider and tens of millions of passenger records from an airline. A single hacktivist using stolen API keys breached at least 14 of 42 targeted organisations and built a mass doxxing platform. Anthropic's own conclusion is the sharpest line in the report: intent, not sophistication, now separates a lone actor from a state sponsored group. That lands the same week Moody's flagged cyber as one of the most pressing exposures on its Emerging Risk Radar and Lockton data showed average premiums fell around 11 percent in 2025 even as incident frequency climbed, so the pricing direction and the threat direction are moving apart on documented evidence rather than projection. On the biological side, Anthropic blocked five cases including a gain of function grant application on chikungunya and work on avian flu mammalian adaptation, but admitted it could not always tell legitimate research from malicious intent, which leaves the dual use coverage question for life sciences liability policies unresolved. What remains unproven is whether this generalises beyond Anthropic's own platform telemetry, since no other model vendor's data is cited and no industry loss figures yet exist.

Accenture finds only 23 percent of insurers scale AI enterprise wide

So whatThis is the concrete benchmark for a portfolio governance leader showing the bottleneck is conversion of pilots to enterprise production, not the volume of use cases already identified.
DeeperThe supplied text is headline level only, so methodology, sample size, region mix, and the exact definition of scaled use are not stated, and those details matter before citing this number externally. Even so, the figure is directionally consistent with the rest of today's set: Mosaic's new AI Lab explicitly positions itself against two years of pilots that never reached production, and the separate Insurance Nerds piece on underwriters notes that AI saves time but decision quality gains still lag. Read together, the pattern is that time savings and pilot proliferation are outpacing governed, measurable production deployment, which is exactly the gap a portfolio review should be pressure testing rather than assuming away. The number is useful less as an industry statistic and more as a prompt to check whether your own program counts pilots or counts production when it reports AI progress internally.

Mosaic Insurance embeds engineers directly in underwriting and claims

Mosaic hired four AI, machine learning and data science specialists into a new AI Lab and Forward Deployed Engineer program embedded in underwriting, claims, finance and operations teams.

So whatThis is a specific operating model choice, embedding builders in business teams rather than running a central pilot factory, that is worth benchmarking against your own AI governance structure even though the scale is still small.
DeeperCo-CEO Mitch Blaser framed the rationale directly: most of the market has spent two years running pilots, and the Lab exists to build AI that runs in production on real data rather than repeat that cycle. The design reuses AI infrastructure across projects and draws on the company's own policy, loss and operational data, with human oversight and validation against business outcomes stated as controls. At four hires and a single London office, this is a signal of intent rather than proof of scale, and it directly counters the Accenture finding elsewhere in today's set that only 23 percent of insurers scale AI enterprise wide by claiming an operating model built from day one to avoid that trap. What is not yet demonstrated is whether the Forward Deployed Engineer model actually converts pilots to production faster than the conventional route, or how business outcome validation is measured in practice.
Watch next: Whether Mosaic reports actual production deployments from the Lab, not just headcount or program launch, within the next two quarters.
