AIG CEO says AI data center risk is maxing out P&C capacity

AIG CEO Eric Andersen told Bloomberg TV that the AI data center buildout is pushing P&C insurers to their limits across construction, cyber, property and liability coverage.

So whatA single data center asset now generates simultaneous demand across four separate lines, which means aggregation and concentration risk is building faster than most carriers' limit management frameworks were designed to track.
DeeperAndersen's actual claim is narrower than the headline suggests. He is not saying AI is unprofitable for insurers, he is saying the industry lacks enough limit capacity to serve the buildout, and he frames AIG's multi line presence in construction, cyber, property and power provider coverage as a competitive advantage rather than a warning to the market. S&P Global Ratings separately called the data center wave a growth opportunity, so the capacity strain and the growth narrative are two sides of the same story. The context matters: Andersen is two months into the CEO role after Peter Zaffino's turnaround pruned AIG's portfolio and reversed years of underwriting losses, and AIG just beat Wall Street expectations in Q2. Yet AIG shares are down 9.3 percent year to date while the KBW Insurance Index is up 8.9 percent, so the market is not yet pricing this as unambiguously good news for AIG specifically. What remains unproven is whether any carrier, including AIG, has a systematic way to see the aggregate exposure across construction, property, cyber and liability books when they all attach to the same physical asset. This connects directly to the Allianz climate warning and the trillion dollar data center investment figure in today's set: three separate signals pointing at the same underwriting blind spot.
Watch next: Watch for any carrier disclosing a data center specific aggregation limit or exposure cap in Q3 filings or earnings calls.

Swiss Re and SAS bundle catastrophe data into underwriting workflow

Swiss Re and SAS partnered to integrate Swiss Re's CatNet catastrophe intelligence with SAS's Insurance Life Cycle Accelerator, giving insurers cat risk data inside existing actuarial and underwriting systems.

So whatThis targets the secondary peril blind spot directly, since Swiss Re Institute found that 99.9 percent of insured catastrophe losses in the US in 2025 came from secondary perils like floods, hail and wildfire rather than major named events.
DeeperThe efficiency numbers driving the press release are vendor projections, not audited results: SAS and Swiss Re forecast a 95 percent increase in decisioning efficiency, a 50 percent improvement in modelling efficiency, and the ability for underwriting teams to assess 40 percent more risks with the same staffing. None of those figures come with a named carrier pilot or third party validation attached, so treat them as marketing math until a customer discloses actual results. The more durable fact in the release is the 99.9 percent secondary peril statistic, which validates something carriers already suspect: historical loss data alone is no longer sufficient for pricing flood, hail and wildfire exposure, and this is why reinsurers are pushing hazard data further into the primary underwriting stack rather than keeping it at the portfolio level. Governance features, including champion challenger model deployment and explainable AI outputs, are pitched as regulatory readiness, which matters given growing supervisory scrutiny of automated underwriting decisions. What is not disclosed is pricing, contract terms, or whether this requires insurers to be existing SAS customers to benefit, which limits how broadly this reaches the market in the near term.
Watch next: Watch for a named carrier publishing actual underwriting capacity or loss ratio results from this integration, not just vendor projected efficiency gains.

AI native TPA launches outsourced contents claims service

Adjusto launched Adjusto Remote, pairing 500 plus field and desk adjusters with its inventory and valuation platform, and cites one job where 2 hours 40 minutes of inventory video covering 1,765 items was processed to a valuation recommendation in about 7 minutes.

So whatIf that processing speed holds at scale, it compresses contents claims cycle time and cost in a segment carriers already outsource heavily to TPAs, which pressures the economics and differentiation of existing TPA relationships.
DeeperCEO Michael Balarezo frames the launch as a response to carriers who like Adjusto's core platform but want the field labor bundled in, which is a direct challenge to the traditional TPA model where the technology behind outsourced work is usually opaque to the carrier. The 7 minute processing claim is a single case example from one 11 room total loss assignment, not a disclosed average across the 500 plus adjuster network, so the real operating question, valuation accuracy and dispute rates at scale, is not answered here. Carriers considering this need to know how FairMatch valuation recommendations compare to adjuster judgment in contested claims, and what quality control exists across a distributed contractor network rather than an employed staff. This sits alongside Sixfold's underwriting partnership with Sollers in today's set as evidence that AI vendors are now competing on claims execution and outsourced labor, not just decision support software sold to existing carrier staff.
Watch next: Watch for a named carrier disclosing cycle time, cost per claim, or dispute rate data from an Adjusto Remote engagement.
