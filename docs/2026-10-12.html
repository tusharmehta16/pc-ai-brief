Title insurer backs mortgage AI agent platform at scale

First American Financial joined a $30 million Series B for Vesta, a loan origination platform whose AI agents review applications, make underwriting decisions, and check closing documents, bringing its total funding to $85 million.

So whatThe headline funding figure is small, the operating proof point is not: Vesta says automated workflows now complete about 40% of tasks on its platform and revenue grew more than 12 times in a year, which is a live benchmark for what agentic underwriting adoption actually looks like at production scale rather than in a pilot.
DeeperConversion Capital led the round, with Pennymac, New American Funding, Citi Ventures, nbkc bank, FirstKey Mortgage, a16z, Zigg and Navitas also participating, and Vesta says lenders using or transitioning to its platform originate more than $100 billion in loans annually. The company reports customer count and headcount each grew more than 50% over the past year alongside the 12x revenue growth, which suggests the 40% task automation figure reflects genuine throughput rather than a demo statistic. First American's rationale is explicit: exposure to mortgage automation workflows that coordinate with settlement and insurance providers, meaning this is a bet on connective tissue between lending, title and insurance processes rather than a pure underwriting play. None of this is P&C underwriting, and nothing in the release addresses loss ratios, error rates, or regulatory findings on the AI agents' decisions, so the 40% figure describes task completion, not decision accuracy or audit outcomes. What it does offer a carrier is an external reference point: a comparable financial services underwriting workflow already runs a sizable share of decisioning through AI agents in production, with investor capital following adoption rather than promises. The open question is whether Vesta's AI underwriting decisions have faced fair lending or regulatory scrutiny, which is the direct analog to how a P&C regulator would eventually look at AI-driven underwriting decisions.
Watch next: Whether any state or federal regulator examines Vesta's AI underwriting decisions for fair lending compliance, since that scrutiny model is the template P&C regulators will likely apply to agentic underwriting.
