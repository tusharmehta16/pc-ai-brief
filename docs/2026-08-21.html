Gallagher Re builds dedicated AI and digital risk practice

Gallagher Re launched a Digital Risk Practice combining AI liability, data center, and cyber expertise, naming Ian Newman global head of Digital Risk, Freddie Scarratt AI liability lead, and Luca Drane Data Centers lead.

So whatA major reinsurance broker is now formally structured to quantify AI and digital accumulation risk across property, casualty and specialty lines, which will shape how much capacity and at what price primary carriers can source for tech-driven exposure.
DeeperThis is an organizational move, not a new product or pricing model. Newman's own framing is instructive: he describes the goal as giving clients 'a clearer view of where digital exposure exists, how it accumulates,' language that admits nobody has that view today. The stated trigger is accumulation risk, the idea that a single technology failure can cascade losses across multiple insureds, portfolios and lines, which is exactly the mechanic behind silent cyber and now behind AI. Nothing in the announcement says whether this practice has already repriced a treaty or simply exists to advise on future ones. It connects directly to two other stories in this set: the IUA's warning that cyber wordings may already carry unintended AI cover is the precise ambiguity Scarratt's new role exists to address, and the reported $5 billion in new data center capacity is the capital-side counterpart to the same underlying exposure. What remains unproven is whether this translates into better terms or simply better paperwork for reinsurers managing their own accumulation.

Co-operators elevates its claims AI architect to CIO

Co-operators named Craig Bran, who built AI and digital tools into its claims service model, as EVP and CIO reporting to CEO Rob Wesseling, replacing Aayaz Pira who had held the role less than a year.

So whatA personal lines carrier that owns its own agent distribution can turn AI-enabled claims into a direct channel advantage over broker-distributed rivals, while its broker-dependent commercial arm stays structurally untouched.
DeeperThe company's own commercial and specialty subsidiary, Sovereign General, writes exclusively through independent brokers and still posted a 24.2% return on equity in Q2 2026, delivered almost entirely through the exclusive-agent parent channel, which suggests Bran's mandate will sharpen rather than erase that channel split. A separate industry study cited in the piece, covering more than 200 brokers and 18 insurers, found Canada's strongest claims operations for 2026 had built AI, automation and digital intake directly into triage, fraud review and settlement rather than layering it on top, meaning every carrier that closes this gap raises the bar a broker's own book gets measured against. What the announcement does not explain is why Pira left the CIO role after less than a year, a governance question worth having an answer to before it gets asked. This is a leadership and org-design signal more than a technology disclosure, no new capability or metric is revealed here, just its elevation to the executive table.
