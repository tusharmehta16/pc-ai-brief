Vendor research flags verification gap in AI underwriting

Clearspeed, a voice-based risk verification provider, published research arguing insurers are automating underwriting and claims decisions faster than they are building infrastructure to verify the information behind those decisions.

So whatIf verification capability lags automated decisioning, misrepresentation and fraud risk get baked silently into growing books of AI-touched business without anyone noticing until claims time.
DeeperThis is a vendor putting out research that happens to justify the exact product category it sells, so the framing deserves scrutiny rather than acceptance at face value. The supplied text gives no survey size, methodology, or named insurers, so there is no way to assess how widespread the gap actually is or whether it is concentrated in specific lines. The underlying structural claim is still worth taking seriously: as underwriting and claims workflows lean harder on automated data ingestion, the human or system checks that used to catch inconsistent applications or fabricated documentation can quietly disappear from the process. This connects directly to the cyber story in today's set, where insurers are reportedly having to rewrite policy language because autonomous AI agents can act on data no one verified first. It also connects to domusIQ's pitch, which is explicitly selling real-time building data as the fix for exactly this kind of verification shortfall in commercial real estate underwriting. What remains unproven is whether Clearspeed's own product closes the gap it describes, or whether this is simply market positioning ahead of a renewal cycle where AI governance questions are becoming standard in due diligence.

Verisk launches database to track AI data centre exposure

Verisk released a US Data Center Exposure Database covering more than 2,500 facilities, citing industry estimates that global data centre insurance premiums could rise from roughly $10 billion in 2026 to $23 billion by 2030.

So whatData centre assets are becoming a new concentration risk inside property portfolios, and carriers need to know whether they are already carrying accumulated exposure to this infrastructure without pricing for it.
DeeperThe headline growth figure, $10 billion to $23 billion in premium, is a market-sizing number aimed at specialty and data centre-focused writers, and it is not the number a diversified P&C carrier should fixate on. The more relevant point, made by Verisk's Jay Guin, is that data centres have quietly become one of the largest concentrations of insured value in the economy, individual facilities can hold billions in assets, and portfolios may already have undisclosed accumulation across geographies. Rob Newbold's framing that AI is 'increasingly a physical infrastructure story' is the useful reframe here: this is a catastrophe and concentration risk problem, not just a new underwriting opportunity. The database itself is a vendor product feeding into Verisk's own Synergy Studio and Touchstone platforms, so this is Verisk selling exposure management tooling, but the underlying risk it addresses, unpriced accumulation of AI-driven infrastructure value, is real and independently verifiable through public data centre build-out figures. What is not yet known is how quickly carriers will actually integrate this data into live accumulation models versus treating it as a nice-to-have overlay. This sits alongside the verification gap story as another instance of infrastructure racing ahead of the risk management tooling built to price it.
Watch next: Track whether reinsurers start requiring data centre accumulation disclosure as a standard cedant submission item.

domusIQ launches AI-led global underwriting business for real estate

domusIQ launched a Bermuda-based risk holding company and a UK MGA hosted on Davies' platform, backed by A-rated capacity of £25 million for property and £10 million for liability through Bridgehaven's fronting model, led by Donald Harrell and a team drawn from Willis Re, Aspen, Liberty International and AIG.

So whatA new, well-capitalised, data-led competitor is entering commercial real estate underwriting with real-time building data as its core proposition, raising the bar for incumbents' own underwriting data infrastructure.
DeeperThe specifics here are concrete rather than promotional filler: named leadership with decades of specialty insurance experience, live underwriting capacity numbers, and a functioning fronting arrangement through Bridgehaven, not just a pitch deck. Harrell's own framing, that real-time building data changes pricing and risk management, is the actual thesis of the business, and it directly echoes the verification gap problem in story 4: domusIQ is positioning its data as the fix for underwriting decisions that currently rely on stale or self-reported property information. The UK MGA went live on 1 August 2026 with plans to expand into the US, EU and Bermuda, so this is an active rollout, not a concept. What is unproven is loss performance, since the business has no underwriting track record yet, and the capacity figures, while real, are modest relative to the global commercial real estate market. For carriers writing CRE risk, the near-term relevance is competitive rather than existential: this is a new entrant testing whether better building-level data actually produces better pricing, and the answer will show up in domusIQ's loss ratios well before it shows up in market share.
